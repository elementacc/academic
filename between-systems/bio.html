    I study how interactive technologies shape the ways we perceive, think and move. My starting point is the body: perception, cognition and action form one continuous way of meeting the world, and every interface intervenes in that loop. Trained in psychology and interaction design, I work between first-person inquiry and empirical study, and I build the interfaces I study. My doctoral research follows how people learn to draw in immersive space, without a page to lean on, and how they form spatial references through their own movement. With clinicians, designers and data scientists I develop navigational interfaces for complex neurosurgical imaging, where what an interface makes visible changes what can be understood. Alongside research I teach, run practice-based workshops and make creative technology projects. Increasingly I ask how systems that take part in thinking and making, AI among them, change the conditions under which we perceive, reason and come to know.
