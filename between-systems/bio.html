    Berlin, Germany

    My starting point is the body: perception, cognition and action form one continuous way of meeting the world, and every interface intervenes in that loop. My doctoral research follows how people learn to draw in immersive space, without a page to lean on, and how they form spatial references through their own movement. With clinicians, designers and data scientists I develop navigational interfaces for complex neurosurgical imaging, where what an interface makes visible changes what can be understood. Alongside research I teach, run practice-based workshops and make creative technology projects. Increasingly I ask how systems that take part in thinking and making, AI among them, change the conditions under which we perceive, reason and come to know.

  2. Natural user interfaces

    Gesture
    Gaze
    Posture
    Whole-body movement

    Methods

    Cognitive task analysis, phenomenologically informed inquiry, longitudinal studies, bodystorming

    My focus is on natural user interfaces: systems that let people interact through gesture, gaze, posture and movement instead of only keyboards, mice and controllers. I look at how these interfaces fit the ways we already move and understand space, and at what changes when the body itself becomes the input.

  3. Explorations

    Eye drawing
    Gaze as a drawing input
    Movement-based input
    Hand, head, foot, full body
    Inclusive interfaces
    Teaching and student projects

    Approach

    Designing from bodies and abilities that standard interfaces overlook

    Most interfaces quietly assume one kind of body and one way of perceiving: hands on a controller, eyes on a screen. My practice questions that norm. I explore inputs such as gaze, posture and whole-body movement, and design with people whose perception, cognition or bodily abilities differ from what technology expects.
